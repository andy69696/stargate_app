How the matching works

Profile. Each placeholder is read as a role family (from its role name, else its practice tag), a SFIA level, an AWS or Azure lean where the role says so, and its client. Named people are profiled from their Kantata roles, grade, business unit, skills ratings and sector experience.

Score. Role family match 40 (practice-only 15) · SFIA level: same 20, one above 12, one below 8 · skills evidence up to 20 (average of the top five ratings in the relevant capability group) · availability over the placeholder's months: 60%+ free 20, 30%+ 10 · knows the account +6 · cost above the planned day rate −8. Employees, suppliers and partners are scored alike.

Policy. Employees first, always: the capacity pass takes placeholders in start-date order and tries employees (right role and level, then stretch) before it will consider a supplier or partner. Filled is an employee with the right role family, same level or one above; Stretch an employee a level below or practice-only; Contingent a supplier or partner because no employee could be found; Gap nobody. Recruit FTE is recommended only where no employee could fill, the work is long term (120+ days or a 6-month engagement) and the role is strategic (cloud, architecture, security, data, development, delivery) on Won or Commit work. Everything else is contingent by exception.

Stacking rule (9 Oct 2026). Someone on a T&M engagement, even at 100%, can still be allocated to an outcome-based engagement. For placeholders on a Fixed Price, Annuity or BU-delivered managed-service engagement (Kantata product group), a person's T&M bookings are ignored when testing availability; absence, internal time and other outcome-based bookings still count. Because UBDS forecasts everyone at 90% of working days, a "full" T&M booking is ~90% and the other ~10% is the leave allowance, not spare capacity — so stacking is capped: a person's total planned load (T&M + outcome + absence) may not exceed 110% of working days (the 10% fixed-price efficiency assumed in the FY27 plan; the cap is a parameter in match.py). People with real free capacity are used before anyone is stacked on top of T&M, and a pick that takes someone above 100% is marked stacked with the T&M load it sits on. Placeholders on T&M engagements keep the full availability test. The Over-utilisation view on the RM dashboard will still show the combined load, by design.

Availability is working days minus everything booked in Kantata (client work, absence, internal) for . Placeholders starting later than that get a provisional pick on profile only. Capacity on a Kantata placeholder is not a commitment: the proposed names are a starting list for the resourcing conversation, not an allocation.

Engagements whose Kantata forecast is held at engagement level (Forecast at this level = Y) rather than built out in detail: no delivery elements, assignments or period phasing behind the number. Cross-referenced to the HubSpot deals with the same code.

Levers to lift margin, quantified from Kantata's forward data and the employee-first policy. Each list is the action queue; nothing here changes Kantata until you say so.

1 · Displace suppliers and partners

Live supplier and partner assignments where an employee with the same role family, within one SFIA level, has the capacity left after the placeholder pass. Saving is the cost-rate difference on the remaining days; margin is at the assignment's billing rate.

▾

2 · Below rate card

Employee assignments billed under 85% of the person's standard rate. Much of this is framework pricing (DWP in particular), so treat the uplift as the ceiling for rate negotiations, not a forecast.

▾

2 · Over-graded on the role

SFIA 6 employees on assignments earning under 30%: either the role is priced a grade low or the person should be swapped for a SFIA 5.

▾

3 · Bench cost, next 3 months

Employees at least 30% free; unrecovered cost is free days at standard cost. The names here are the supply for levers 1 and 4.

▾

2 · Supplier and partner contracts ending

Within 120 days and still assigned: the natural moment to exit, replace with an employee, or renegotiate the rate.

Rules applied · Demand

▾

Scope. Kantata engagements that are not Lost. Managed-service product groups (IoC Managed Services, SoC Managed Services, Annuities) are hidden unless switched to Included; Pipeline engagements are excluded from Not detailed. Basis is the Kantata P3 forecast (Won Closed, Contracted, Commit, Most Likely, Best Case) unless narrowed by the status chips.

Generic roles. A generic role is an unnamed resource line on an engagement (for example #Cloud SFIA5 - Employee). Days = Kantata forecast hours ÷ 8, phased evenly across the line's dates and counted inside the chosen window. Started = the start date has passed with nobody named. Recruit / recruit TBC is Kantata's own recruitment flag on the line.

Capacity pass (Allocation options). Employees first: generic roles are taken in start-date order and matched to employees before any supplier or partner is considered. Filled = an employee with the right role family at the same level or one above; Stretch = one level below or practice-only; Contingent = a supplier or partner where no employee could fill; Gap = nobody with the capacity and profile. Availability is working days less everything booked in Kantata. Stacking: someone on a T&M engagement can still take outcome-based work, capped at 110% total load. Recruit FTE is recommended only where no employee could fill, the work is long term (120+ days or a six-month engagement) and the role is strategic on Won or Commit work. The Action dropdown records the Resource manager's choice in this browser only; it does not change Kantata.

Not detailed. Engagements with no resource assignments, matched to their HubSpot deal by engagement code. Red = the figure exists in one system only; amber = the two disagree (value more than 10% apart, start dates more than 14 days apart, or Kantata status ≠ HubSpot stage); plain = aligned. Variance £ = Kantata minus HubSpot. For a single financial year, values are pro-rated by calendar day across the deal's or engagement's dates. Rows are ordered by earliest start date. Explain narratives use the row's own facts only.

Rules applied · Margin

▾

Margin watch. Margin = (revenue − cost) ÷ revenue on named assignments in the chosen window, where revenue is the billed day rate and cost is the employee's internal cost rate or the supplier/partner rate; the Actual / Forecast switch picks the months. The threshold defaults to 40% (the target GPM) and can be moved with the slider: red below the threshold, green or blue at or above it. By engagement shows the top 18 engagements by revenue with their blended margin; Individuals below threshold lists people whose blended margin across their assignments is under the threshold, with their lowest single assignment.

Margin improvement actions. Displace suppliers and partners: live supplier or partner assignments where an employee with the same role family, within one SFIA level, has capacity left after the generic-role pass; saving = (supplier cost rate − employee cost rate) × days left, with the new margin recomputed; sorted lowest margin first. Supplier and partner contracts ending: contracts ending within 120 days while still assigned, the natural moment to exit, replace with an employee or renegotiate. Colour bands on this tab are fixed: 40% and above green, below 40% red.

Explain. Each Explain narrative is generated from that row's own figures only (rates, costs, days, dates, the threshold) and is written as a So what and a Recommended action for an executive reader.
